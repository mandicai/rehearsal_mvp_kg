Directional Motion Sketches

Black shapes are subjects/entities; the orange rectangle is the camera frame. Each sketch isolates a directional relationship that can later be instantiated with real objects such as people, trees, charts, machines, or places.

1. Reveal →
A → B
Pan from one entity to another to reveal a relationship, consequence, or hidden context.
2. Return ←
B → A
Reverse direction to return from context or evidence to the original subject, often creating reflection.
3. Converge
Bring together
Two entities move toward a shared point: collaboration, synthesis, agreement, or multiple causes producing one outcome.
4. Diverge
Separate paths
A shared origin splits into distinct trajectories: contrast, branching outcomes, disagreement, or differentiation.
5. Approach
Inspect
The camera closes distance around an entity: “this deserves closer attention.”
6. Retreat
Contextualize
The camera opens outward from an entity: “this belongs to a larger situation or system.”
7. Ascend ↑
Escalate
Upward motion can imply growth, increasing scale, progress, emergence, or movement toward abstraction.
8. Descend ↓
Ground / drill down
Downward motion can imply grounding, drilling into detail, decline, or moving from abstract to concrete.
9. Orbit ↻
Examine perspectives
Movement around a subject suggests scrutiny, complexity, or changing perspective without abandoning the subject.
10. Cross / Intersect
Encounter
Independent trajectories cross: encounter, collision, unexpected connection, or two processes affecting one another.
11. Countermotion
Tension
Subject and camera move in opposing directions, creating resistance, separation, tension, or instability.
12. Enter → Hold → Exit
Event / transition
An entity enters the frame, occupies attention, then leaves: arrival, intervention, interruption, or temporal transition.
